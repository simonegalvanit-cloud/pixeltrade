Getting started

Introduction

pixeltrade is a trading interface for perpetual futures that settle fully onchain.

Every order you place through pixeltrade goes to Hyperliquid, a blockchain built for trading that runs its order book, margin system and settlement onchain. pixeltrade is the screen you trade on. Hyperliquid is where the trade actually happens.

What pixeltrade is and isn't

  • It is an interface: charts, an order book view, an order panel, positions, and tools that help you understand leverage and risk.
  • It isn't a custodian. pixeltrade never holds your funds. Your collateral sits in your own Hyperliquid account, controlled by your wallet.
  • It isn't the counterparty to your trades. Your orders match against other traders on Hyperliquid's order book.
  • It doesn't run its own matching engine, price oracle or liquidation system. Those are Hyperliquid's, and the rules are the same for every interface.

Current status

Trading through pixeltrade isn't live yet. The site currently shows live market data from Hyperliquid's public API, a practice order panel with a demo balance, and a trade simulator. Nothing you do on pixeltrade today places a real order.

The trading mechanics in these docs (margin, funding, liquidations, fees) are Hyperliquid's rules, which every pixeltrade order will follow. Anything specific to pixeltrade that isn't built yet is marked planned and may change before launch.

The life of an order

  1. You set it upMarket, side, size, leverage, margin mode and optional take profit or stop loss in the order panel.
  2. pixeltrade shows the numbersOrder value, margin required, estimated liquidation price and fees, before anything is signed.
  3. Your wallet signsThe signed order is sent to Hyperliquid's API. Trading actions are gasless.
  4. Hyperliquid matches itAgainst resting orders on the onchain book. The fill, your position and your margin are recorded onchain.
  5. pixeltrade shows the resultYour position, live PnL, liquidation price and funding, read straight from Hyperliquid.

Where the data comes from

Prices, order books, candles, funding rates, open interest and volume on pixeltrade come from Hyperliquid's public info API and websocket. pixeltrade doesn't generate or adjust any of it. The tutorial and the trade simulator on the homepage are the only places with demo numbers, and both are labelled.

Getting started

Quick start

This is the flow for when trading opens. Steps 1 and 2 you can do today.

  1. Get an EVM walletRabby, MetaMask, Coinbase Wallet or any WalletConnect wallet. Write down your recovery phrase offline and never share it.
  2. Get USDCUSDC is the collateral for perps. Hyperliquid accepts USDC from Arbitrum and several other chains. You'll need a little of the source chain's gas token for the deposit itself; trading after that is gasless.
  3. Connect to pixeltradeClick Connect wallet and approve the connection.
  4. Enable tradingSign one gasless message so pixeltrade can submit orders for your account.
  5. Approve the builder feeA one-time signature that sets the maximum fee pixeltrade may charge. See Fees.
  6. Deposit and tradeDeposit USDC to your Hyperliquid account, pick a market, set leverage, place your first order.

New to leverage? Run through the interactive tutorial and the trade simulator first. They use a demo balance.

Trading

Perpetual futures

A perpetual future ("perp") lets you bet on an asset's price going up or down without owning the asset, and without an expiry date.

  • Long: you profit if the price rises.
  • Short: you profit if the price falls.
  • Collateral is USDC. Profits and losses are settled in USDC.
  • There's no expiry. A funding payment every hour keeps the perp price close to the real asset price.

How profit and loss work

PnL (long)  = size × (exit price − entry price)
PnL (short) = size × (entry price − exit price)

size is in coins, e.g. 0.01 BTC

Example: you long 0.01 BTC at $100,000. BTC rises to $104,000. Your PnL is 0.01 × 4,000 = +$40, before fees and funding. Leverage doesn't change this number. It changes how much margin you had to put up to get it.

While a position is open, its PnL is unrealized. It becomes realized when you close.

Trading

Leverage & margin

Initial margin

Margin is the collateral locked to back a position. The margin to open a position is:

initial margin = position size × mark price / leverage

So $1,000 of BTC at 10x needs $100 of margin. At 20x it needs $50. Leverage can be any whole number from 1 up to the market's maximum, which differs per asset and is shown in the leverage picker.

  • Leverage is only checked when you open a position. After that, you're responsible for watching your margin.
  • You can increase the leverage of an open position without closing it.

Withdrawing margin

Any action that takes margin away from positions, other than trading (for example a withdrawal or removing isolated margin), must leave at least:

max(initial margin required, 10% × total position value)

Maintenance margin

Maintenance margin is the minimum you must keep to avoid liquidation. On Hyperliquid it's half of the initial margin at the market's max leverage:

maintenance margin rate = 1 / (2 × max leverage)

40x max → 1.25% of position value
20x max → 2.5%
3x max  → 16.7%

Some markets use margin tiers, where the requirement steps up as a position gets larger.

Trading

Order types

pixeltrade's order panel covers market, limit and TP/SL orders, the same set used in the homepage tutorial. Hyperliquid supports more, listed here for reference.

TypeWhat it does
MarketFills immediately at the best available prices in the book.
LimitFills only at your price or better. Rests in the book until filled or cancelled.
Stop market / stop limitBecomes a market or limit order once the price reaches your trigger.
Take market / take limitLike a stop, but for the profit side: triggers when price moves in your favour.
ScaleSeveral limit orders spread across a price range.
TWAPSplits a large order into smaller pieces sent at intervals (at least 30 seconds apart), each with max 3% slippage.
Trailing stopA stop that follows the best mark price reached and triggers when price retraces by your chosen distance.

Order options

  • Reduce only: can only shrink or close a position, never open or flip one.
  • Good til cancel (GTC): rests in the book until filled or cancelled.
  • Post only (ALO): only added to the book as a resting order; it's rejected rather than filling immediately.
  • Immediate or cancel (IOC): whatever can't fill right away is cancelled.

Maker and taker

An order that rests in the book and gets filled later is a maker order. One that fills immediately against resting orders is a taker order. Market orders are always taker. Takers pay higher fees than makers.

Trading

Take profit & stop loss

A take profit (TP) closes your position at a target. A stop loss (SL) closes it before a loss gets bigger.

  • TP/SL orders trigger on the mark price, not the last trade.
  • By default they're market orders with a 10% slippage tolerance once triggered.
  • You can set a limit price instead to control slippage. The trade-off: in a fast move, a limit TP/SL can trigger and then sit unfilled.

On a position vs on an order

Set on an open position: by default it closes the whole position when triggered. If you set a specific size, it stays that size even if the position changes.

Set on a new order (attached TP/SL): it's sized to that order and only becomes active once the order fully fills. If you cancel a partly filled order, the attached TP/SL is cancelled too, so add a fresh TP/SL to the part that filled.

Your stop loss must sit before your liquidation price. A long's stop must be above the liquidation price, a short's below it. Otherwise you're liquidated first and the stop never fires. pixeltrade's order panel warns you when this happens.

Trading

Funding

Funding is a payment between longs and shorts, every hour, that keeps the perp price close to the real asset price.

  • If the rate is positive, longs pay shorts. If negative, shorts pay longs.
  • It's peer to peer. Neither Hyperliquid nor pixeltrade takes a cut.
  • It's paid every hour, added to or taken from your balance.
  • It's capped at 4% per hour.

How the rate is set

The rate has two parts: a fixed interest component of 0.01% every 8 hours (0.00125% per hour), and a premium that measures how far the perp is trading above or below the spot oracle price. The premium is sampled every 5 seconds and averaged over the hour.

funding rate (8h) = average premium + clamp(interest − premium, −0.05%, +0.05%)
hourly payment    = position size × oracle price × (8h rate ÷ 8)

Example

You hold a $10,000 BTC long and the hourly rate is +0.0013%. You pay 10,000 × 0.000013 = $0.13 that hour. A short of the same size receives it. Over a day at that rate, about $3.12.

The markets table on the homepage shows each market's current hourly rate, and the BTC card counts down to the next payment.

Trading

Mark & oracle prices

Three prices matter, and they can differ, especially in fast markets.

PriceWhat it isUsed for
Last / mid priceThe price trading on Hyperliquid's book right nowWhat you see in the order book and what market orders fill near
Oracle priceA weighted median of spot prices on major centralized exchanges, computed by Hyperliquid's validatorsFunding payments
Mark priceCombines external exchange prices with Hyperliquid's own bookLiquidations and TP/SL triggers

Because liquidations use the mark price, a brief spike on one order book alone doesn't liquidate you. It also means that in a sharp move, the book price and the mark price can be noticeably apart.

Trading

Liquidations

You're liquidated when your account equity falls below the maintenance margin. Your position is then closed for you.

What happens

  1. Market close on the bookHyperliquid sends market orders to close the position. If that restores the maintenance requirement, any collateral left over stays yours. There's no liquidation fee.
  2. Partial for large positionsFor positions over 100k USDC, only 20% is sent at first, followed by a 30-second cooldown.
  3. BackstopIf equity falls below two thirds of the maintenance margin without the book closing it, the liquidator vault takes the position over. In this case the maintenance margin is not returned to you.
  • Cross: a backstop liquidation transfers all your cross positions and cross margin. With no isolated positions, your account ends at zero.
  • Isolated: only that position and its margin are affected. Your other positions are untouched.

The exact formula

liq_price = price − side × margin_available / position_size / (1 − l × side)

side = 1 for long, −1 for short
l    = 1 / maintenance leverage   (maintenance leverage = 2 × max leverage)
margin_available (isolated) = isolated margin − maintenance margin required
margin_available (cross)    = account value − maintenance margin required

Fees

Fees

A trade on pixeltrade has two fees: Hyperliquid's trading fee and pixeltrade's builder fee. Funding is separate and goes to other traders, not to anyone's fee account.

Hyperliquid trading fees

Set by your rolling 14-day volume, assessed daily (UTC). Spot volume counts double toward your tier.

Staking HYPE gives a further discount from 5% (over 10 HYPE) up to 40% (over 500,000 HYPE). Large maker volume can earn rebates instead of paying fees.

pixeltrade builder fee

pixeltrade earns through Hyperliquid's builder codes: a fee added to orders you place through pixeltrade, processed onchain as part of Hyperliquid's fee logic.

  • Before your first trade you sign a one-time approval setting the maximum fee pixeltrade may charge. It must be signed by your main wallet.
  • You can revoke that approval at any time.
  • Hyperliquid caps builder fees at 0.1% on perps. pixeltrade can never charge more than that, or more than you approved.
  • A wallet can have up to 10 builder approvals active at once.

pixeltrade's builder fee rate will be published here and shown in the order panel before you sign anything. It hasn't been set yet.

Example

A $1,000 market order (taker) at tier 0 pays 1,000 × 0.045% = $0.45 to Hyperliquid, plus pixeltrade's builder fee. Opening and closing are each a trade, so a round trip pays fees twice.

Account

Deposits & withdrawals

Deposits and withdrawals move USDC in and out of your Hyperliquid account. pixeltrade shows your balance as soon as it lands.

  • Deposit: USDC from Arbitrum is the most common route. Hyperliquid also accepts USDC from several other chains and some other assets, which then need swapping to USDC to use as perp margin.
  • Gas: you need the source chain's gas token for the deposit. Trading on Hyperliquid itself is gasless.
  • Withdraw: back to your wallet. Depending on the chain and method, small fees may apply.
  • Margin rule: you can't withdraw margin that open positions need (see Withdrawing margin).

Because your funds live on Hyperliquid, not on pixeltrade, you can always reach them through any Hyperliquid interface, including app.hyperliquid.xyz, even if pixeltrade is offline.

Risks

Leveraged trading can lose you your entire deposit, fast. Please read this.

  • Leverage: at 10x, a 10% move against you wipes out the margin, and liquidation comes a little before that.
  • Liquidation: positions can be closed at a loss without your input. In a backstop liquidation you also lose the maintenance margin.
  • Volatility & slippage: prices can gap. Market orders and triggered stops can fill worse than expected, especially in thin markets.
  • Funding: holding a position costs or earns funding every hour, which adds up on long holds.
  • Mark vs book: in fast markets the mark price used for liquidations can differ from the book price you see.
  • Technology: blockchains, bridges, wallets and interfaces can have bugs, outages or exploits.
  • Regulation: perpetual futures aren't available to residents of some jurisdictions. You're responsible for following the rules where you live.

Nothing in these docs or on pixeltrade is financial, legal or tax advice. Only trade with money you can afford to lose.

FAQ

Is pixeltrade an exchange?

No. It's an interface. The exchange is Hyperliquid: orders match on its onchain order book and positions settle there.

Who holds my money?

You do. Your collateral is in your own Hyperliquid account, controlled by your wallet. pixeltrade can't withdraw it.

What if pixeltrade goes offline?

Your positions and funds stay on Hyperliquid. Open any other Hyperliquid interface, such as app.hyperliquid.xyz, with the same wallet to manage or close them.

Why do I sign a builder fee approval?

It's how Hyperliquid lets interfaces charge a fee. You set the maximum, Hyperliquid enforces it (and its own 0.1% cap on perps), and you can revoke it any time.

Is the max leverage the same as on Hyperliquid?

Yes. pixeltrade reads each market's max leverage directly from Hyperliquid. The BTC badge on the homepage is that live value.

Are the prices on the homepage real?

The terminal card, order book, markets table and ticker are live from Hyperliquid. The tutorial panel and the trade simulator use a demo balance and simulated price paths, and are labelled that way.

Can I trade today?

Not yet. pixeltrade is in private preview. Trading opens after testing on Hyperliquid's testnet.
